Correlation & RANSAC bad-channel detectors: PREP vs EEGLAB clean_rawdata (MATLAB) vs eegprep (Python)

2026-10-02; plan (§8) and first results (§9) updated 2026-10-05 · for step2_bad_ch_time_detection.py: bad_ch_by_correlation and bad_ch_by_RANSAC, implemented in commit a50f46a
Sources. PREP: Bigdely-Shamlo et al. 2015 + EEG-Clean-Tools v0.57.0 (commit 0914132). EEGLAB: clean_rawdata d4b143f; its .m files are identical to eeglab2026.0.0/plugins/clean_rawdata. EEGPrep: Delorme et al. 2026 + eegprep 0.3.0 source.
Method. Independent readers went through every source line by line, and their claims were re-checked adversarially against the source. MATLAB R2026a and Python were then run on the same sub-02 data. Citations are file:line in those repos.

1. Bottom line

  • RANSAC is the same algorithm in both code bases. PREP's RANSAC is a port of Kothe's clean_channels.
    • Shared core: the same spherical-spline code; 50 random subsets of 25% of the channels, shared by all targets and windows; the per-sample lower median of the 50 predictions; and an uncentred cosine in 5-s windows.
    • Differences: thresholds (0.75 vs 0.8), the time rule (more than 40% vs more than 50% of the recording), the RNG, which channels may predict, and where it runs. PREP runs inside its robust-reference loop; EEGLAB runs it once, with no re-referencing.
  • The two "correlation" detectors use different statistics and play different roles.
    • PREP runs it on every call, next to RANSAC. The score is the 98th percentile of |r| over all channels, which is about the 3rd-best partner at 129 channels. A channel is bad if that score is below 0.4 in more than 1% of 1-s windows.
    • EEGLAB uses clean_channels_nolocs only as a fallback when RANSAC cannot run. It asks whether at least k = ceil(0.1·C) partners reach |r| ≥ 0.45 (k = 13 at 129 channels), in 2-s windows. A channel is bad if it fails in more than 50% of the time.
  • EEGLAB's MATLAB and Python versions are aligned VERIFIED.
    • Same inputs and parameters flag the same channels on both paths, with bit-identical random draws and subsets.
    • Numerical differences are ≤ 1e-10. The closest any value came to its threshold was 7e-5.
    • The two diverge only on malformed inputs (§5).
  • Your suspicion is correct: without locations, HF noise is skipped in BOTH versions.
    • The HF z-scores are computed before the location check and then discarded on the error path. LineNoiseCriterion is never passed to clean_channels_nolocs.
    • Test: a channel with HF z = 15.3 was removed when locations were present and kept when they were absent, in both languages.
    • No comment, docstring or commit message says this is intended. The history points to a leftover: the location-free function dates from 2010, the location+HF function was added in 2014, and the older one was kept as a fallback.
    • The "more than 75% removed" guard also reroutes to this HF-blind path.
  • Neither code base follows the PREP paper exactly. Both carry defects we should not copy: the target is in its own predictor subset, the subset sampler is biased toward the middle of the channel list, the last window is dropped, the filter band depends on the sampling rate, the cosine is uncentred, and some paths skip or switch detectors silently (§6).
  • Their thresholds do not transfer to our data. EEGLAB at default settings flagged 12 of 129 channels by RANSAC (plus 3 by HF) on 120 s of sub-02, with nothing injected (plain average reference, 0.5 Hz high-pass).

Subsets / RNG
  • 50 subsets, one set shared by all targets and windows.
  • A private RandStream (mt19937ar, seed 435656) on each call; the sampler deletes each picked channel from the list.
  • Deterministic, but subset membership reshuffles whenever the pool changes.
  • 50 subsets, shared.
  • rng('default') resets MATLAB's global stream; this equals numpy RandomState(5489).
  • Commit 2ee0662 (2025-12-21) switched MATLAB to a swap-based sampler that matches Python. EEGLAB releases from 2022-03 to 2025-12 therefore draw different subsets, and releases before 2022-03 were not reproducible.
Sampler biasBOTH Both samplers use round((n−1)·U), which gives the end positions half the probability. In the test, the last channel in the list was drawn at 0.133 instead of 0.248.
Target in own subsetBOTH Yes. Each subset is mapped onto all channels, including the subset's own members. The PREP paper (p. 9, left column) describes the predicted channel as excluded from its subset; neither paper discusses the issue.
  • With the fixed seed, each channel is in 5–21 of the 50 subsets. Self-weight on EGI-128: median 0.58, range 0.23–0.97.
  • Effect on sub-02 (EEGLAB code): median r rises by +0.088 for poorly predicted channels vs +0.013 for the rest. E4 and E49 are removed only when the target is excluded. In a PREP test, a bad channel's score went from 0.654 to 0.709.
findNoisyChannels.m (calc_projector); clean_channels.m:181
SplineSAME CODE Kothe/Perrin spline: λ = 1e-5, m = 4, Legendre series summed to tol = eps (at most 500 terms). Positions are scaled to unit length without centring. PREP's repair step uses a different model (eeg_interp, λ = 0, 7 terms). For comparison, MNE interpolate_bads uses m = 4, 50 terms, α = 1e-5 and centres on a fitted sphere.
AggregationSAME At each sample, the 50 predictions are sorted and the 25th is kept (round(end/2)). This is the "lower median": the usual median would average the 25th and 26th. On sub-02 this changed r by a median of 6e-5 and changed no decision. Because the median is taken sample by sample, the result stitches together different subsets' predictions and is not any single spatial interpolation.
SimilarityBOTH Uncentred, signed cosine Σxy/(‖x‖‖y‖), i.e. Pearson r without removing the window means. It is biased by each window's DC offset and drift, so with the high-pass off it flags DC-offset channels. 7. Evidence from our data (exploratory: sub-02, plain average reference, no prior bad-channel removal)

  • EEGLAB at defaults (100–220 s):
    • RANSAC flagged E10, E16, E19, E20, E29, E30, E37, E87, E105, E112, E113 and E127.
    • HF flagged E55, E62 and Cz.
    • Injected faults also lowered a neighbour's score: E4's bad windows rose from 9/23 to 12/23.
  • PREP-style score (3rd-best |r|; 1–40 Hz, 2-s windows, 300–500 s): median 0.91, 1st percentile 0.56. An injected independent-noise channel scored about 0.17. A 0.4 cutoff therefore fires only on near-total loss of signal.
  • Median signed r with the 6 nearest neighbours: 1st percentile 0.08, so it spreads channels out much more. Some central channels (E30, E13, E87) had weak or negative r with neighbours 2–3 cm away.
  • MNE spline noise gain ‖w‖₂: leave-one-out median 0.47, versus 0.87 with 25% subsets. At the rim and around the eyes it is 0.81 (E126/E127) and 0.71 (E48/E119), so those channels are inherently less predictable.
  • Leave-one-out weights are dominated by the 6 nearest neighbours (summed weight ≈ 1.06). A single bad neighbour carries weight ≈ 0.18, and a bridged twin would predict its target well.
  • Reference dependence: the spline residual x − W·xS does not depend on the reference, because each row of W sums to 1 (verified for MNE). r does depend on it.

8. Implementation plan

Decisions as of 2026-10-03; every detail is confirmed. Implemented in step2 and committed as a50f46a (2026-10-04); first results are in §9.

Both detectors

ItemSpecification
Roles
  • Correlation catches intermittent loss of shared signal: a channel that sometimes shows only its own signal, e.g. from broken contact.
  • RANSAC catches channels that are persistently inconsistent with the field the other channels predict.
  • Bridged electrodes are out of scope for now.
ReferenceRequire average reference: raise if any EEG channel, bad or not, is entirely zero, as in smallness, std and BBnoise. Exclude existing bads and non-EEG channels. Evaluate Cz as a normal channel.
BandFilter an internal copy of the whole recording before windowing. The robust-reference loop and the other detectors stay at 0.3 Hz.
  • High-pass: 1 Hz, MNE's default FIR (1 Hz passband edge, −6 dB at 0.5 Hz, 3.3 s long).
  • Low-pass: 45 Hz passband, 50 Hz stopband, from _lowpass_45_hz, which _prep_hf_ratio also calls (Hamming FIR sized from the 5 Hz transition, 0.66 s long).
  • _band_limit applies both filters and returns their combined reach, used to widen the masks.
WindowsComplete non-overlapping 2-s windows from the first sample; the trailing partial window is dropped.
MaskingCompute the temporary voltage mask on the 0.3 Hz input. Widen each masked span on both sides by the filters' spread, then skip windows that overlap a mask or a BAD annotation. All fractions count retained windows only.
  • Widen by the filters' combined reach, computed from their lengths: about 2.3 s each way. That is 1.65 s for the high-pass, applied once, plus 0.66 s for the low-pass, applied forward and backward. The plan first said 2.0 s, because it counted the low-pass as a single pass.
Place in the loopIn robust_average_reference_inplace, after BBnoise. Develop and test them inside the loop directly; the data they test there are not interpolated.
  • Correlation runs before RANSAC, as in PREP. Channels it flags then don't serve as RANSAC predictors.
  • ransac_method="threshold" (default) or "robust_z" chooses RANSAC's flagging rule.
Flat electrodesNeither detector can catch them. Average referencing turns a constant into minus the mean, which is exactly what Cz becomes: sub-10's E119, flat in 412 of 414 raw windows, has r = 1.000 with Cz in all of them. bad_ch_by_flatline must catch them. It missed them while it ran after line-noise removal and the 0.3 Hz high-pass, which break exact equality. Since 2026-10-03 it runs on the unfiltered data right after cropping, and it now flags sub-10's E119. Tabled: electrodes flat in no more than 25% of windows still pass, e.g. sub-17's E119 (18%).
CyclesNo new mechanism. The existing cycle rule only compares flagged sets, so it doesn't matter which detector flags a channel. It needs every detector to be deterministic; for RANSAC that comes from the fixed seed.
GuardsRaise in house style; never skip a detector or switch algorithms silently. Raise on:
  • missing, NaN or zero-norm positions;
  • fewer than 32 predictors per target;
  • a constant evaluated channel in any retained window, in the input or the band-limited copy, or a constant RANSAC median prediction;
  • no retained windows;
  • sfreq ≤ 100 Hz.

Correlation detector (PREP-style)

ItemSpecification
Per-window scoreFor each evaluated channel, compute its signed Pearson r with every other evaluated channel. The score is the 98th percentile of those values.
  • The self-correlation is excluded, and the quantile is numpy's default (linear). At 110–128 partners this falls between the 3rd and 4th best partner, as in PREP.
Window ruleA window is bad if the score is < 0.4 (strict).
  • Zero variance raises a ValueError instead of being handled. The check covers any evaluated channel in any retained window, both in the band-limited copy, where r would be undefined, and in the input, since filtering turns an exactly constant input into round-off noise that would otherwise pass silently. The same rule applies in RANSAC, also to a constant median prediction. It should never happen: no recording had a constant channel after average referencing (0 of 13,827 windows in 33 recordings). If it ever does, the error forces an inspection.
Channel ruleA channel is bad if it is bad in strictly more than 1% of retained windows. In a 14-min recording that means 5 or more of about 415 windows.
DetailsHouse fields, plus the per-window score (channels × windows).

RANSAC detector

ItemSpecification
PredictorsA fixed 32 channels per subset: round(0.25 × 129), 25% of all EEG channels, so the count is the same in every subject. Each subset is drawn uniformly from the non-bad channels, never including the target, using a fixed-seed local RNG. There are 50 subsets per target.
  • Subsets are drawn separately for each target, as the PREP paper describes. Excluding the target then leaves every target exactly 50 predictions. The shared-bag alternative leaves a varying ~35 after excluding the subsets that contain the target.
  • The RNG gives each target 50 random orderings of all other EEG channel names, fixed regardless of which channels are bad. Each subset takes the first 32 non-bad channels of its ordering. Subsets are then uniform, reproducible, and stable across passes: a newly bad channel is just replaced by the next one in the ordering.
  • Build the subsets and spline weights once per call; together they take a few MB. Compute the 50 predicted traces window by window, so they are never held for the whole recording.
PredictionUse MNE's spherical spline (_make_interpolation_matrix, origin as in interpolate_bads) from each subset to the target. Take the per-sample median of the 50 predicted traces: the usual median, from sorting float32 predictions, about 4× faster than a float64 median, with r changing by about 1e-7. Then compute one Pearson r per window between the channel and that median trace.
FlaggingImplement both rules behind a method argument and compare them on test runs. The details hold r and z for both rules either way.
  • Threshold: a window is bad if r < 0.75; a channel is bad if it is bad in more than 25% of retained windows.
  • Robust z: pool r over the channel × retained-window matrix. z = (median − r) / (1.4826·MAD), so that a low r gives a high z. A window is bad if z > 5; a channel is bad if it is bad in more than 25% of retained windows.
  • On a 120-s sub-02 crop, z = 5 fell at r = 0.72, and the two rules flagged nearly the same channels (18 vs 20). §9 compares them across all recordings.
DetailsHouse fields, plus:
  • per-window r and z (channels × windows);
  • the subsets;
  • the input, band-limited and median-predicted waveforms in flagged windows;
  • each channel's median noise gain ‖w‖₂ over its subsets, as a read-out for later diagnosis of rim bias; not used for flagging.

Tabled

  • The balance for frontal and face electrodes, including std flagging most frontal channels.
  • RANSAC's rim and periocular bias: look at more subjects first.
  • A median-over-windows rule: revisit together with std.

  • Cycles come from channels right at the 1% correlation cutoff. On sub-02, E45, E98 and E102 have 4–5 bad windows out of 414, against the more than 4.1 required, so they flip between passes.
  • RANSAC finds persistent outliers: sub-02's E87, E112 and E30 have median r of 0.27–0.48.

That is a steep periocular field the spline can't follow: the rim bias, tabled.
  • Polarity inversion (synthetic test): correlation misses it, because the inverted channel finds positive partners among channels anticorrelated with its true neighbours. RANSAC catches it, and a test covers this.
  • Runtime per call on a 14-min recording: correlation about 5 s, RANSAC about 23 s (37 s with details).

Predictability across all 33 recordings (first pass: only the initial bads interpolated into the robust mean)

  • High r is normal here. Median RANSAC r over all channel-windows is 0.925–0.987, with a median of 0.965.
  • Why: Pearson r weights frequencies by power, and most 1–45 Hz power is slow and spatially smooth.
    • PC1 explains 28–87% of the variance. In 26 recordings it sits on frontal or periocular channels, with most power at 1–4 Hz (eye activity).
    • With PC1 removed, median r is still 0.90–0.98.
    • sub-03, the highest at 0.987: about 91% of its power is at 1–4 Hz, predicted at r = 0.991, while 30–45 Hz is predicted at 0.908 but carries about 1% of the power. It still has a long low tail: 10% of channel-windows are below 0.78, and 18 channels have median r below 0.9.
  • Ceiling effect. r cannot exceed 1, so its spread shrinks as the median approaches 1 (Spearman −0.92 across recordings).
    • The raw-r robust-z cutoff therefore ranges from r = 0.56 to 0.90 and is strictest in the most predictable recordings (sub-03: 28 flags).
    • The fixed 0.75 rule instead flags more in less predictable recordings (Spearman −0.42; sub-11: 22 flags, sub-07: 0).
    • Flags per recording: median 11 for the fixed rule and 13 for robust z, 10 of them in common.
    • Robust z on Fisher-transformed r removes the coupling but flags nothing at z = 5, so it would need its own calibration.
  • No sign of widespread bridging. MNE's compute_bridged_electrodes reports 1,153 pairs in sub-03 and 3,606 in sub-20, against none in 30 recordings. Most are 7–17 cm apart, too far for salt bridges: its cutoff hit its 16 µV² limit. sub-10's 6 pairs (adjacent, about 1.2 µV²) look genuine.
  • What RANSAC measures here: mostly whether a channel follows the slow, largely ocular field. A channel noisy only above about 8 Hz may still score high; HFnoise covers that.

Open

  • Fixed threshold vs robust z. Leaning: the fixed threshold, which is interpretable and avoids the ceiling effect. Its cutoff level and the 25% rule still need calibrating.
  • The 1% correlation rule, given the flips at its edge.
